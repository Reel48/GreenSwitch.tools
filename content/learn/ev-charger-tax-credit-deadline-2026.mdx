## This Is the Last One Standing

The federal EV purchase credits are already gone — the $7,500 new-vehicle credit (Section 30D) and the $4,000 used-vehicle credit (Section 25E) both ended September 30, 2025. (For the full picture, see our [2026 guide to what's left of the EV tax credit](/learn/2026-ev-tax-credit-guide).)

One federal incentive is still alive: the **Section 30C alternative fuel refueling property credit** — the home EV charger credit. But not for long. It **expires June 30, 2026**, and after that date there are no federal EV-related credits of any kind.

The honest answer up front: if you qualify and you've been putting off a charger install, this is genuinely your last window. If you don't live in an eligible area, the deadline doesn't change anything for you.

## What the Credit Covers

The 30C credit is worth **30% of the total cost of your charging equipment and its installation**, capped at **$1,000** for a residential charger. "Total cost" is broader than just the hardware:

| Eligible cost | Counts toward the credit? |
|---|---|
| Level 2 charger hardware | Yes |
| Electrician labor | Yes |
| Electrical panel upgrade (if needed for the charger) | Yes |
| Permit and inspection fees | Yes |
| A second charger at the same home | Separate $1,000 cap |

To hit the full $1,000 credit, your eligible costs need to total about $3,335 — which a Level 2 install requiring a panel upgrade can easily reach.

## The Catch: Your Address Has to Qualify

This is where most people fall out. The credit only applies if your home sits in an **eligible census tract**, which means one of two things:

- A **low-income community**, or
- A **non-urban area** (broadly, rural census tracts)

A large share of suburban addresses do **not** qualify. Before you spend money to beat the deadline, confirm your specific address is in an eligible tract — the IRS and the Department of Energy both publish lookup tools, and your installer can usually check for you. Don't assume; verify.

## What "Placed in Service" Means With Two Weeks Left

The deadline is not when you order the charger or sign a contract — it's when the property is **placed in service**. For a home charger, that generally means it's installed and operational on or before **June 30, 2026**.

With the deadline this close, that has real consequences:

- A charger you order today but that gets installed in July does **not** qualify
- Permitting and inspection timelines vary by jurisdiction — some take days, some take weeks
- Keep every receipt: equipment, labor, permits, and the panel upgrade if applicable

If your installer can't credibly commit to completing the job before June 30, the credit shouldn't be the reason you rush — a botched or delayed install isn't worth chasing $1,000.

## Is It Worth Rushing?

Run the math honestly. The credit is worth up to $1,000, but only 30% of what you actually spend. If a basic install runs you $1,200, your credit is $360 — nice, but not life-changing. If you need a $3,500 install with a panel upgrade, the full $1,000 is a meaningful offset.

Three questions decide it:

1. **Were you going to install a Level 2 charger anyway?** If yes, beating the deadline is found money. If you're only doing it for the credit, the net cost rarely justifies it.
2. **Is your address eligible?** No tract, no credit — full stop.
3. **Can it be placed in service by June 30?** Get that commitment in writing.

A home charger pays for itself over time regardless of the credit, because home charging costs a fraction of public DC fast charging. See how the numbers shake out with our [EV Charging Cost Calculator](/calculators/ev-charging-cost).

## After June 30

Once the 30C credit lapses, the federal government is out of the EV incentive business entirely. What remains is **state and utility programs** — many utilities still offer rebates for home charger installation and discounted off-peak charging rates, and those are unaffected by the federal sunset. A quick call to your utility is worth more than chasing a federal credit you may not be eligible for.

## The Bottom Line

The 30C home charger credit is the last federal EV incentive, and it disappears June 30, 2026. It's worth up to $1,000, but only if your address is in an eligible census tract and the install is completed in time. If you check both boxes and were already planning a Level 2 charger, move now. If not, focus on the operating-cost savings instead — and check what your utility offers.
