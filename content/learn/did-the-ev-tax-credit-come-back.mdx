## The Current Answer: No

If you're searching this, you want a straight answer, so here it is: **as of 2026, the federal EV tax credit has not come back.** There is no federal credit for buying a new or used EV. The new-vehicle credit (Section 30D, $7,500) and used-vehicle credit (Section 25E, $4,000) both ended for vehicles acquired after **September 30, 2025**, and no law has reinstated them.

This page exists to give you the current status without the speculation and clickbait that surrounds the topic. We update it when the facts change.

## What's Actually True Right Now

- **New EV credit (30D):** Ended. Not available in 2026.
- **Used EV credit (25E):** Ended. Not available in 2026.
- **Leasing credit (45W):** Ended. No federal credit is built into EV leases — see [leasing an EV in 2026](/learn/leasing-ev-after-tax-credit-2026).
- **Home charger credit (30C):** Expired **June 30, 2026** — it was worth up to $1,000 in eligible areas, and a charger placed in service by that date can still be claimed. Details in our [charger credit guide](/learn/ev-charger-tax-credit-deadline-2026).

The one legitimate way to claim a *federal* EV credit today is a **grandfathered 2025 purchase** — if you had a binding contract and payment by the September 30, 2025 deadline. See [how to claim a grandfathered credit](/learn/how-to-claim-grandfathered-ev-credit-2025-taxes).

## Why the Confusion Keeps Coming Up

A few things keep this question alive:

- **Automaker discounts.** Several manufacturers replaced the lost credit with their own price cuts and rebates. People see "$7,500 off" and assume the federal credit is back — it isn't. That's the automaker's money. See [the EV deals replacing the tax credit](/learn/ev-deals-after-tax-credit-2026).
- **State programs.** Many states still offer their own EV incentives, which are easy to mistake for the federal one. See [state EV incentives in 2026](/learn/state-ev-incentives-2026).
- **The "200,000-unit cap" myth.** Old articles claim certain brands still qualify under a sales cap. That rule was repealed in 2022 and doesn't apply — full explanation in [which EVs still qualify in 2026](/learn/which-evs-still-qualify-tax-credit-2026).

## What Would Have to Happen for It to Return

A federal EV credit can't simply reappear on its own. Bringing one back would require **Congress to pass new legislation and the President to sign it.** That's a real, multi-step political process — not a scheduled event. As of 2026, no such bill has been enacted, and there's no automatic trigger that reinstates the old credit.

The practical takeaway: don't make a purchase decision based on a credit "coming back." Decide on the numbers in front of you today, and treat any future credit as a bonus if it ever materializes.

## The Bottom Line

The federal EV tax credit has not returned, and the last federal incentive — the home charger credit — expired June 30, 2026. No federal EV incentives remain. What's worth your attention now is at the automaker, state, and utility level.
